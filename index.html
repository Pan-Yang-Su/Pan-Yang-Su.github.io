Pan-Yang Su

PhD Candidate · Advised by Prof. Shankar Sastry · Electrical Engineering & Computer Sciences, UC Berkeley

I study game theory and mechanism design for multi-agent systems. I aim to develop modeling frameworks and analytical tools to study interactions among diverse agents, such as humans, robots, and algorithms, in dynamically changing environments. Some applications include electric vehicle charging, airspace allocation in advanced air mobility, multi-robot task allocation, and traffic routing. Earlier work focused on resource allocation and federated learning in wireless networks.

Research

Mechanism design for societal-scale infrastructure

Current focus

I design auction- and incentive-based mechanisms for allocating scarce, shared infrastructure — electric vehicle charging slots, vertiport capacity for advanced air mobility, and road/airspace usage — where participants have private valuations and may act strategically.

Game-theoretic models of traffic and routing

Ongoing

With collaborators at Berkeley, I study equilibrium behavior and fairness in arc-based traffic assignment models, and how tolling policies shape congestion outcomes.

Resource allocation in wireless and federated systems

Prior work, National Taiwan University

My earlier research addressed strategy-proof resource allocation for multicast wireless networks and auction-based user selection for federated learning.
