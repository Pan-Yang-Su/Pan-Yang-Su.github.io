Research

My research focuses on multi-agent systems, particularly those arising in engineering applications. I study how to design mechanisms for systems involving heterogeneous, strategic agents whose decisions are interconnected and evolve over time. Drawing on game theory, mechanism design, and optimization, I develop mathematical frameworks and analytical tools to characterize strategic interactions and design mechanisms that align individual incentives with system-wide objectives.

My research explores three interconnected themes:

  1. Heterogeneity and resource allocation. Real-world systems involve agents with diverse and potentially multidimensional preferences. I develop mathematical frameworks, particularly using optimal transport and convex analysis, to characterize equilibria and socially optimal allocations in such systems. I also study fundamental trade-offs between efficiency and fairness in transportation systems. Related: Congestion Games with Heterogeneous Valuations; Average Unfairness in Routing Games.
  2. Dynamic mechanism and market design. In many systems, agents make strategic decisions that affect their future opportunities and payoffs. I investigate how mechanisms and markets can account for intertemporal incentives and coordinate resource allocation over time, with applications to electric vehicle charging and airspace allocation. Related: Two-Stage Mechanism Design for EV Charging; Incentive-Compatible Vertiport Reservation; Dynamic Auction Mechanisms in a Closed-Credit Economy.
  3. Learning in games and adaptive incentive design. Agents may learn and adapt their behavior in response to their experiences and incentives. I study the resulting learning dynamics and investigate how adaptive incentive mechanisms can guide decentralized behavior toward socially desirable outcomes. Related: Arc-Based Traffic Assignment: Equilibrium Characterization and Learning; Dynamic Tolling in Arc-based Traffic Assignment Models.
